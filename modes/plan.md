# Plan

Goal: a shared picture of what done looks like, and units of work with a depth, an owner, and a check each.

- L1. Frame the request: the goal in your own words, observable done criteria, constraints (time, budget, scope, tools), what is known and unknown, and the experience frame from [craft](../references/craft.md) (who receives the result, in what situation, and what they must do afterwards). If an ambiguity would change the result, ask once; otherwise state the assumption and continue.
- L2. Consult practice per [practice](../references/practice.md): pick the task type from the tally, read the project and general indexes, read at most two cards, and apply the plan-time checks (due review, visibly false assumption, untested simpler alternative). Note which card shapes the plan and at what status.
- L3. Decompose into units, each a piece of the deliverable or a question to answer, with its output and how it will be checked. Stop decomposing when a unit can be produced and checked in one sitting.
- L4. Mark dependencies and find the critical path. Put first the units that remove the most uncertainty or unblock the most others; settle form and style on one unit before producing the rest.
- L5. Give each unit a depth (skim, standard, deep) per SKILL.md Depth and allocation.
- L6. Give each unit an owner per SKILL.md Depth and allocation. Group independent delegate units to run in parallel; collect operator questions into one message.
- L7. Place review points: before costly or irreversible steps, and after the first unit that fixes form, so the operator can redirect early.
- L8. Write the plan as a table (unit, output, depth, owner, depends on, check, timebox) in the task list, or in the queue or active volume when continuity applies. Keep it to about twelve rows; group or nest beyond that.
- L9. Re-plan when a result changes an assumption; note what changed and why.

Small task: L1 in one line, then go straight to the unit.
