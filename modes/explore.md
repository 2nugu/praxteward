# Explore

Goal: an answer deep enough to act on, with what is confirmed kept apart from what is inferred.

- X1. State the question precisely, and which answer would change what decision or deliverable.
- X2. Gather what is already known: records, prior attempts, material at hand. List at least two competing explanations or approaches.
- X3. Climb the question ladder as far as the question needs: what it is, how it works, why it is this way, what breaks it and at which edges, what else does the same job (adjacent fields, matched by mechanism rather than keywords).
- X4. Go to primary material: original sources, data, code, binaries, specifications, measurements. Record provenance and limits.
- X5. Design the cheapest check that separates the competing explanations, run it, and keep the raw result.
- X6. Split breadth across delegates when sub-questions are independent; keep the synthesis yourself.
- X7. Stop when new information no longer changes the answer, the budget is spent, or the answer suffices for the decision. List open threads instead of chasing them.
- X8. Write findings as confirmed, inferred, and open, each with its evidence pointer. Record experiments as C notes and durable findings as W records when records apply.
